FEVER
A fever is a temporary increase in body temperature, often due to an illness.
Normal body temperature is around 98.6°F (37°C).
A fever is generally defined as a temperature above 100.4°F (38°C).
High fever above 103°F requires immediate medical attention.
Fever with stiff neck, severe headache, or rash may indicate meningitis - emergency.
Fever in infants under 3 months always requires immediate medical evaluation.
Common causes: viral infections, bacterial infections, heat exhaustion, certain medications.
Treatment: rest, hydration, acetaminophen or ibuprofen for adults.

HEADACHE
Headaches are pain in any region of the head.
Tension headaches are the most common type - feel like pressure around the head.
Migraine headaches cause severe throbbing pain, often with nausea and light sensitivity.
Warning signs requiring emergency care: sudden severe headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, headache after head injury, headache with confusion or vision changes.
Cluster headaches cause severe pain around one eye.
Chronic headaches occurring more than 15 days per month need medical evaluation.

CHEST PAIN
Chest pain is always a serious symptom requiring immediate evaluation.
Heart attack symptoms: crushing chest pain, pain radiating to arm or jaw, sweating, shortness of breath.
Call emergency services immediately if chest pain is accompanied by shortness of breath.
Not all chest pain is cardiac - can be due to muscle strain, acid reflux, or anxiety.
Pleuritic chest pain worsens with breathing and may indicate pneumonia or pulmonary embolism.
Any new chest pain should be evaluated by a doctor within hours.

BREATHING DIFFICULTY
Shortness of breath can range from mild to life-threatening.
Emergency signs: severe shortness of breath at rest, blue lips or fingertips, inability to speak in full sentences.
Common causes: asthma, COPD, pneumonia, heart failure, anxiety, anemia.
Asthma attacks with no relief from inhaler require emergency care.
COVID-19 can cause breathing difficulties - seek medical care if oxygen levels drop below 94%.

ABDOMINAL PAIN
Location of pain helps identify cause: right upper quadrant suggests liver/gallbladder, left lower quadrant suggests colon issues.
Appendicitis: pain starting around navel moving to right lower quadrant, with fever and nausea - emergency.
Severe sudden abdominal pain may indicate perforation or rupture - emergency.
Pain with vomiting blood or black stools requires immediate evaluation.
Mild cramping with diarrhea often indicates gastroenteritis - manage with hydration.

DIABETES WARNING SIGNS
High blood sugar symptoms: excessive thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision, fatigue.
Low blood sugar symptoms: shakiness, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat.
Diabetic ketoacidosis: fruity breath, extreme thirst, vomiting - emergency.
Patients with diabetes should monitor blood sugar regularly.
Target blood sugar: 80-130 mg/dL before meals, less than 180 mg/dL after meals.

HYPERTENSION
Normal blood pressure is below 120/80 mmHg.
Stage 1 hypertension: 130-139/80-89 mmHg.
Stage 2 hypertension: 140+/90+ mmHg requires medication.
Hypertensive crisis: above 180/120 mmHg - emergency, seek immediate care.
Symptoms of dangerously high BP: severe headache, vision changes, chest pain, difficulty breathing.

PEDIATRIC EMERGENCIES
Fever above 100.4°F in infants under 3 months - immediate emergency.
Difficulty breathing in children - emergency.
Severe dehydration signs: no tears, dry mouth, no urination for 8 hours - emergency.
Seizures in children lasting more than 5 minutes - emergency.
Rash with fever in children should always be evaluated by a doctor.

MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS
Suicidal thoughts or self-harm ideation - immediate crisis intervention needed.
Severe anxiety attacks with chest pain should be evaluated to rule out cardiac causes.
Psychosis symptoms: hallucinations, severe confusion, disorganized behavior - emergency.
Depression with inability to function for more than 2 weeks needs professional evaluation.

COMMON INFECTIONS
Urinary tract infections: burning urination, frequent urination, pelvic pain.
Upper respiratory infections: runny nose, sore throat, mild fever - usually viral, rest and fluids.
Skin infections with red streaking, warmth, and fever may indicate cellulitis - needs antibiotics.
Sexually transmitted infections should be tested and treated promptly.
